# figS05_mixture_timing

Figure S5. Computational cost of G-MoQ and direct mixture maximum likelihood under the two-component mixture model. Panels a-c give the cost of one matched panel of 100 simulated variants at three sample sizes, for the whole G-MoQ pipeline with the pooled or the 1,000-block jackknife covariance and for direct mixture maximum likelihood. **a**, Time to fit the estimates and their standard errors (s). **b**, Wall time of the whole process (s). **c**, Peak resident memory (MiB). Each G-MoQ time includes the one-off baseline mixture fit, Stage 1 and Stage 2 for the panel, so it is dominated by costs not repeated per variant (per-variant costs, Fig. 2c). Each point is the arithmetic mean of three timing repeats of one method at one sample size, on log axes; no error bars are shown. **d**, Implied cost of 1.2 million variants at 400,000 participants on one core, from the per-variant costs of Fig. 2c; the value printed is CPU-hours, and bars start at 0.01. Direct mixture maximum likelihood needs 3,354 CPU-hours; G-MoQ Stages 1 and 2, 5.3 CPU-hours (631 times faster); Stage 2 alone given the Stage 1 output, 3 CPU-minutes (about 68,000 times faster). One-off per-trait costs are excluded. No statistical tests are shown. ML, maximum likelihood; SE, standard error; MiB, mebibyte; CPU, central processing unit.

Source data: results/08_simulations/summary, results/03_testosterone/speed.
